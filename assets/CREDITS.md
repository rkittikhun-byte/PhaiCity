## Texture packing and conversion

- `color.jpg`: JPEG quality 82; 4:2:0, except Leaf001 uses 4:4:4 to retain leaf-edge colour.
- `normal.jpg`: OpenGL normal, JPEG quality 88, 4:4:4; no vector-channel subsampling.
- `orm.jpg`: JPEG quality 80, 4:4:4, **R = ambient occlusion, G = roughness, B = metalness** (glTF convention). Three.js samples the appropriate channel; the image is never tagged as sRGB. All three slots share a cached texture when their physical repeats match.
- Missing source AO is packed as 255, missing metalness as 0. A material only assigns channels present in its original scan; materials with scalar metalness and no source metalness map retain that scalar, rather than multiplying it by the default zero channel.
- `alpha.webp`: lossless WebP decoded from the original Leaf001 opacity JPEG; leaf cutouts are not recompressed lossily.
- Cement roofing shares Concrete Wall 006's ORM for roughness and Corrugated Iron's ORM for AO, preserving both sources' original world-scale repeats.
- Surface Imperfections 001's water-smudge mask remains the glass/water roughness source, now in the ORM green channel.
- All original source URLs, authors and CC0 licenses above still apply to these converted derivatives. The 1K HDRI and OFL font files are unchanged.
- Dev tool: `src/render3d/dev/convert-textures.ts`, using the dev-only `sharp` dependency. Run `npm run textures:convert -- --input RAW --output NEW` with Node 22.18+; RAW has one source directory each, containing original `color.jpg`, `normal.jpg`, `roughness.jpg` and optional `ao.jpg`, `metalness.jpg`, `alpha.jpg` (PNG inputs also work). Keep original provider downloads outside shipped assets, never recompress the already-converted JPEGs. NEW must not exist.
- Optional 512-pixel variants are deferred; 1K is the only shipped level. Add them with a distance/quality selection policy when the city renderer exists, rather than downloading unused variants now.

## Adaptations

- `roof_cement_tile`: concrete colour and roughness from Concrete Wall 006, corrugation normal/AO from Corrugated Iron; metalness is zero. This is a composite approximating grey Thai fibre-cement roofing, not a scan of Thai double-wave tiles. A later roof model should build its corrugated silhouette in geometry.
- Plaster and cement coats neutralise the scan colour and retain its grain in the shader, so white plaster and seeded shophouse pastels read as paint rather than brown raw scan colour. The re-encoded scan retains its grain and original dimensions.
- Road paint uses only plaster micro-normal/roughness/AO; the colour stays white or yellow. Curb red/white bands are generated in code over that surface.
- Glass uses the Surface Imperfections 001 water-smudge mask as roughness and its normal at low strength, with physical transmission; no opaque colour map.
- Water uses the same CC0 roughness mask, a generated periodic ripple normal, and Thai river/flood tints. This is a water shader material, not a photograph of water.
- Thai signs use generated canvas colour/emission maps and plaster micro-normal/roughness/AO. Lit windows share this neutral micro-surface.
- Leaf001 provides opacity for double-sided tropical leaf cards. Leaves vary by model tint, not by copying the material.
- Fabric Canvas uses the neutral beige Cotton Jersey scan as a coarse cloth base; its knit is an approximation of woven awning fabric. Its original provider-labelled 1K maps are 1024 × 1025, preserving the measured scan aspect; the other bundled surface maps have a 1024-pixel longest edge.
